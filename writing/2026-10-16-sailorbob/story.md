
# A teacher's website and archive, rebuilt and handed back

> A WordPress site in Australia, two YouTube channels, and a decade of recordings nobody could search. What it took to turn that into a site the owner runs herself, for the price of the domain name.

Sailor Bob Adamson taught nonduality in Melbourne for decades and died in February 2025. His website was a 2018 WordPress build on an Australian host, fourteen pages, with his recordings scattered across two YouTube channels, a Facebook page, and a handful of old MP3s. The person who carries on his weekly meetings needed it kept alive and made useful, and did not want a second job running it.

The site is now at www.sailorbobadamson.com, rebuilt as a static site and served from a free Cloudflare account in the owner's own name. She pays for the domain registration and nothing else. The old hosting lapses at its next renewal.

**What a visitor gets.** Meeting times in Melbourne and US hours, with a calendar feed anyone can subscribe to; the weekly repeat rules are checked across the Australian daylight-saving switch so a Sunday meeting stays a Sunday meeting in Pittsburgh. A books page, and her books linked from every talk page. A one-to-one booking link. A newsletter signup that posts to her mailing-list account with no script on the page.

**The archive.** Every recording on both channels, 645 videos and about 20 GB of audio, was downloaded and run through speech recognition on a GPU at home, with two cloud services taking the tail. Thirteen Facebook live meetings and twelve old site recordings joined them. The result is 681 talk pages, one per recording, each with the video embedded, a note that the text is machine-made, and a full-text search across all of them that runs in the browser from a prebuilt index. There is no server or database behind it, so there is nothing to patch. A reader can find the one talk where Bob used a particular phrase, which was not possible before, from anywhere.

**What carried over.** All fourteen original URLs still resolve, one as a redirect, so years of search ranking and inbound links were kept. The homepage title, the sitemap, and the canonical URLs were fixed on the way. Search Console's first reports after the move showed the redirects it should show and nothing it should not.

**Handing it back.** The site source and the transcripts live in two private repositories she has access to, with the secrets kept out of both. A shared Drive folder holds the how-to guides in her words and mine: how to retitle a listing, how to send the monthly letter, how to add a meeting. Two people can update the site.

**What stayed out.** Recordings from third parties were not republished; their hosts own them. Recordings removed at the family's request were not restored or transcribed. The private Facebook group's material stays private. The transcripts carry people's first names as spoken, with the owner's agreement.
